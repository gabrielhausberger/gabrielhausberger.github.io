These are some late night thoughts. Quick upfront note: I find it intriguing that while I write this, at 2am, a party is going on in the university. I am not into partying, and this was a tiring day, so I didn't go. 

I was looking at career opportunities; I am always worried about my career. While overthinking my options, I ended up looking at other universities (somehow, this is a repeating pattern with me), and ended up looking at Westlake University. The university is very impressive, with such modern infraestructure. I started looking at the Linkedin profile of its students, and the outcomes are equally impressive, with many direct-entry PhD students at the best universities worldwide. However, would this be biased? Are these the outlier Chinese students that you find at every university? The average students perhaps are not that present or recommended on Linkedin. Still, I am inclined to believe the university outcomes are actually insane, regardless of potential bias.

Would I have preferred that university? This pops in my mind all the time, with different universities. I feel like I am never satisfied, but at the same time, I never got to see all opportunities available to me. I applied to one university, and got in. At one point during IB, I remember being so desperate to the point I thought "I just need to get into a university, doesn't need to be very good". The moment I got the offer, I was happy it was unconditional and accepted it imediatelly, believing I had gotten the best opportunity I ever could. But then, my IB scores were released, and I scored higher than expected. While seemingly confusing, scoring so well led to so much self-doubt. Could I be at somewhere better? Am I letting golden opportunities aside? Should I reapply? But if so, isn't it excessively risky? I am afraid of being so ambitious that I not only do not win more, but lose what I already won.

I also believe costs influence my thoughts. It is hard not to think about the weight I put on my parents; how they also have a life, they also worked hard and deserve to get rewarded, but they reduce it so that I can come to this university. I feel selfish, and I think it is selfish. I would understand if my parents demonstrated unhapiness over how I postpone their retirement, increase their workload, so that I can come to this university. Westlake and many others that I considered in the past would not cause this. I try to force myself to believe that because of the damage I cause on my parents, I will perform better, so that I can get a high-paying job and pay them back soon. Still, it hurts.

This is repeated inside my mind at least a few dozen times every week: life already began. This is life; there is no more waiting for it to begin. Is it correct to live a dull life now for the chance of living better later? Maybe I should have more fun. What period of my life will matter the most? Am I sacrificing memories — my most valuable asset — for nothing? I can't test both out to see what way of living is better. I must choose. So, I bet on this life.

While writing, I deviated completely from the theme I wanted to introduce, "internet bubbles", but I will try starting it. I got to this thought while looking at the profile of Westlake University alumni. Specifically, a student whose profile picture in Linkedin is an anime girl picture. Isn't it interesting? A direct entry PhD student in one of the world's best universities using an anime picture. Still, the idea that such style transmits to me feels somehow comforting. It is an aesthetic that I can't explain, but I see it in other places, and its just so tightly close. Everything feels like a bubble, so connected. After that student, the next profile I saw was of a student at my current university. He uses anime profile pictures in some of his social media profiles. His aesthetic gives me the same feeling, as if these two were, in the internet, part of the same close group.

Honestly, I need to think more to write about it, and I am tired. Maybe I can write solely about this thought later, with more time and deliberation. So this is it for today.
